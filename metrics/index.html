Metrics

Measuring an infrastructure factory

A factory that cannot state its own lead time is a ticket desk with better tooling. Six metrics, each with a counterpart on the software side, and the arithmetic that shows why lead time is the one that matters.

Metrics

Measure it like a factory

The software-factory crowd brings numbers: pull requests per week, tokens per engineer, 3 to 5x2. An Infrastructure Factory needs its own set, the equivalent of DORA for environments. Six are enough to start, and each has a counterpart on the software side.

Little's law for environment requests

0.6In flight · factory

At 40 requests a day and a lead time of 3 days, 120 environment requests are open at any moment. Through a factory with a lead time of 20 min, fewer than one is.

Work in progress = arrival rate × lead time. Little's law holds for any stable queue, whatever is in it. The inputs are yours; the arithmetic is the only claim.

What to measure

Measure outcomes, not tokens

Counts that are easy to collect are easy to game. Three measures survive that test.

Human interventions per environment

How many times a person had to act between intent and hand-over. The counterpart of Lloyd's human interactions per pull request in the software factory.36 Zero is the target; the trend is the metric.

Lead time

From intent to a compliant environment, measured per contract. It is the one number that tells a software factory whether the floor keeps up.

Zero-touch rate

The share of requests fulfilled with no human in the path. An agent at 3am only ever gets this share.

Environments created, tokens spent and pull requests raised are activity, not outcomes. Palmie makes the Goodhart point for the software factory: once a count becomes the target, the factory optimises the count.41 Count environments and you get environments. Measure lead time and interventions, and you get a factory.
